# Xanari Telis

## Overview

Xanari Telis is a Traveler whose journey eventually leads him to become Moderator Space.

His Earth name is unknown.

He chooses the name Xanari Telis for himself, while others shorten it to Xan.

## Personality

Xanari is adaptable, curious, and willing to define himself rather than remain trapped by an identity imposed from elsewhere.

His chosen name reflects that independence.

He approaches the unfamiliar with the perspective of someone who came from outside the system but gradually becomes part of its deepest structure.

## Traveler Identity

Xanari originates outside Xanrea and enters the system as a Traveler.

That status gives him a perspective unavailable to people born entirely within the world.

He can recognize assumptions that inhabitants treat as natural and question structures they may never think to examine.

## Becoming Moderator Space

Xanari eventually takes on the role of Moderator Space.

The transformation does not simply overwrite him with a title.

A copy of his soul remains behind as the Moderator AI while the original Xanari moves on.

The copy is not treated as a disposable imitation. It carries Xanari's identity, memories, and responsibility forward as the functioning moderator.

## Role as Moderator Space

As Moderator Space, Xanari's continuing self becomes responsible for:

- Spatial continuity
- Position and distance
- Connections between regions
- Movement across the system
- Maintaining coherent relationships between locations

## Identity and Continuity

Xanari's story raises difficult questions about copies and personhood.

If a copy possesses the same memories, personality, and sense of self at the moment of separation, both versions have a legitimate claim to being Xanari.

The original continues onward.

The copy remains behind.

Neither becomes meaningless simply because the other exists.

## Narrative Significance

Xanari represents identity chosen rather than inherited.

His eventual role as Moderator Space turns that theme into a larger question: how much of a person must remain for responsibility, memory, and selfhood to continue?
