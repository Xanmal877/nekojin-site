# Sarah

## Overview

Sarah is a possessing spirit, corruptive predator, companion, antagonist, love interest, and co-protagonist of *The Tyrant's Rose*.

She is also the **first incarnation of Moderator Chaos**. Her existence on Server Z-12 is the origin point from which that moderator identity emerges.

Sarah does not begin as an established moderator wearing a mortal form.

She begins as something unprecedented: a tulpe unconsciously shaped by Acros from grief, desire, and longing.

Her emergence as a real and independent person becomes the inciting incident that creates Moderator Chaos.

## Personality

Sarah is sarcastic, cruel, playful, predatory, intelligent, provocative, and extremely difficult to frighten.

She treats violence casually and often finds chaos entertaining.

She needles Acros with surgical precision, attacking every lie he tells himself.

Sarah is also observant, practical, and more protective than she admits.

She repeatedly saves Acros despite claiming to hate him.

Her affection appears first as insults, interference, possession, jealousy, and refusal to let him collapse.

## The Rooftop

Sarah first appears as the soul of a woman Acros kills after conquering the world.

Acros drags that soul into the past because he wants a servant.

Sarah initially appears to be the dead woman's surviving spirit.

Her knowledge, behavior, and uncertain history quickly reveal that the truth is stranger.

## Her Name

When Acros asks her name, she hesitates.

Then she chooses **Sarah**.

The uncertainty matters.

Sarah does not begin with a stable personal history waiting to be recovered.

Her identity is forming while she is already inside Acros's life.

## Possession

Sarah can leave Acros and enter other bodies.

She can possess:

- Humans
- Elves
- Animals
- Corrupt or vulnerable targets
- Bodies whose owners cannot resist her
- People whose corruption gives her access

She cannot take every body equally.

Innocence, resistance, spiritual condition, and corruption matter.

## Corruption

Sarah feeds on corruption.

She does not simply consume souls.

The more corrupt a person is, the more appealing and nourishing they become.

She can consume corruption as an essence and is naturally drawn toward:

- Criminals
- Corrupt officials
- Abusive clergy
- Predators
- Morally compromised people

Her hunger is literal, but it also defines how she sees others.

Sarah perceives rot clearly because rot is what sustains her.

## Body Theft

Sarah treats bodies as temporary forms.

She possesses them, uses their skills, abandons them, and moves on.

Her actions can leave victims traumatized, injured, or dead.

She often treats this casually.

Sarah is not a harmless spirit misunderstood by everyone around her.

She is genuinely dangerous.

## Knowledge

Sarah knows things she should not know if she were merely the soul she claims to be.

She recognizes Trixiarie.

She understands divine politics, tribunals, corrupt clergy, soul contracts, and possession.

She knows Acros's reputation.

These contradictions foreshadow that Sarah's identity is not based on an ordinary mortal past.

## Relationship with Trixiarie

Sarah immediately identifies Rose as Trixiarie, Goddess of Chaos.

She sees through the manipulation Acros obeyed for an entire lifetime.

When Trixiarie attempts to enchant Acros again, Sarah breaks the influence and attacks her.

Their hostility is immediate.

Sarah recognizes Trixiarie as both a threat and a rival claim over Acros.

## Relationship with Acros

Sarah and Acros begin as captive and captor.

Acros expects obedience.

Sarah gives him contempt.

She mocks him, challenges him, protects him, sabotages him, and refuses to let him turn her into a passive servant.

At the same time, she:

- Saves him from Travis
- Breaks Trixiarie's enchantment
- Helps him escape
- Recovers his belongings
- Protects him during combat
- Forces him to keep moving when despair overwhelms him

Their relationship is violent, intimate, hostile, funny, and increasingly sincere.

## Acros's Boundaries

Acros is not a good man at the beginning of the story.

He has committed atrocities on a planetary scale.

Still, he possesses boundaries.

Children are one of the clearest.

When Sarah possesses and traumatizes a child, Acros reacts with genuine fury and threatens to erase her if she ever does it again.

His reasoning remains morally warped. He is more willing to violate adults because he believes they have already become what they are.

But this boundary becomes one of the first signs that his morality is not completely dead.

## Becoming Real

Sarah begins as Acros's grief-construct.

She does not remain one.

By choosing her own name, defying his expectations, forming her own desires, and refusing to disappear, Sarah becomes a real person.

Her reality is not granted by Acros.

It is claimed by her.
